Why It Matters

Your customers are already searching for you.

84%

of consumers searched online for a local business in the last three months.

72%

look at three businesses or fewer before deciding who to use.

45%

now use ChatGPT or other AI tools to find local business recommendations.

Source: BrightLocal consumer surveys, 2026. Their samples are mainly US consumers, so treat the exact figures as a guide rather than a UK measurement.

Most people make up their minds quickly and from a short list, so the businesses that appear in that first search are the ones that get the call. Social media helps people discover you, but your profile lives on someone else's platform, under their rules. A website is the one place online that belongs to you, and it's where Google, and increasingly AI assistants, go to find out what you do, where you work and whether you're worth recommending.

That last part matters more every year. When ChatGPT searches for local businesses, BrightLocal found that business websites made up 58% of the sources it showed. If your site doesn't clearly explain what you offer, those tools have nothing to point people towards.

The Process

From first conversation to live site.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    We talk through your business, who your customers are and what you need the site to do. I look at the businesses you compete with and the words people use when they search for what you offer.

  2. 02

    Structure

    I map out the pages, so each service or location has a page that matches what people search for, and plan the content each page needs to answer their questions properly.

  3. 03

    Design

    The site is designed around your brand, so it looks and feels like you rather than a template with your logo dropped in. You see it and give feedback before anything is built.

  4. 04

    Build

    Every site is hand-coded, which keeps it fast, light and free from the bloat that slows template builders down. It's built to work properly on phones first, because that's where most people will find you.

  5. 05

    Search setup

    Before launch, every page is set up for search engines and AI answer tools, from page titles and descriptions to the structured information that tells Google exactly what your business is.

  6. 06

    Launch

    Your site goes live, gets submitted to Google, and you get a walkthrough of how everything works, including how enquiries reach you.

SEO & AEO

Built for the searches that bring in work.

SEO (search engine optimisation) is how you show up on Google. AEO (answer engine optimisation) is how you show up when someone asks ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews or a voice assistant for a recommendation. Every site I build is set up for both.

For search engines

  • Pages planned around the exact phrases your customers search, including the towns and areas you cover
  • A clear title and description written for every page, so your listing makes people want to click
  • Structured data that tells Google your business type, services, contact details and service area
  • Fast loading, with images compressed and only loaded when they're needed
  • A sitemap submitted to Google Search Console so new pages are found quickly
  • Proper headings and image descriptions, which also make the site usable for people with screen readers

For AI answers

  • Plain-language answers to the questions your customers ask, written so AI tools can quote them accurately
  • FAQ sections marked up so search engines know exactly which question each answer belongs to
  • Consistent business details across your site and Google Business Profile, so AI tools trust what they find
  • An llms.txt file, a short summary of your business written for AI crawlers
  • Optional articles that answer the bigger questions in your industry, the kind people now put to AI tools before they buy

One honest note: nobody can promise you the top spot on Google, and anyone who does is guessing. What I can promise is a site that gives search engines and AI tools every reason to understand, trust and recommend your business.

Questions

Before you ask.

How much does a website cost?

A simple single-page website starts at £200. Extra pages and features such as galleries, bookings, online selling or interactive tools are quoted on top, depending on what the site needs to do. You can get an estimate in about two minutes using the project brief tool.

What does the price include?

The price covers the design and code of your website. Your domain name, hosting and any paid third-party tools, such as booking or payment services, are separate costs, and I explain each one before work starts so there are no surprises.

Can you guarantee my website will rank first on Google?

No, and nobody can honestly promise that. What I can do is build every page so search engines and AI tools can read, understand and trust it, which gives your business the best possible footing in the searches your customers make.

Can my website take bookings or payments?

Yes. Bookings and payments are handled through established services such as Cal.com and Stripe, built into your site, so card details never pass through the website itself. Those services charge their own fees, which I'll explain up front.
