How Much Does Social Media Management Cost in the UK?

Real 2026 price bands, what actually moves the number, and how to tell a fair quote from a padded one.

By Emma Winnard · Founder, AEAN Studios · Updated September 2026

Most small UK businesses pay somewhere between £250 and £1,500 a month for social media management. Light-touch posting on a couple of platforms sits at the lower end. Full-service work with original content, video and active community management sits at the top. The number depends far more on what's included than on who you hire.

Every agency seems to quote a slightly different figure for this, which makes it hard to know whether you're being quoted fairly. The honest answer is that there isn't one correct price, because "social media management" covers work that can range from scheduling posts you've already written to building an entire content operation from scratch. What follows is a realistic breakdown of where the money actually goes.

Typical UK price bands

These bands reflect the range seen across UK freelancers and agencies for small business work in 2026. Treat them as a starting point for comparison, not a fixed menu, since scope varies from provider to provider.

TierTypical monthly costWhat it usually includes
Light-touch / freelancer£250–£800Scheduling on 1–3 platforms, basic captions, client supplies most images
Boutique or regional agency£400–£1,500Multi-platform management, custom graphics, monthly reporting, some content creation
Full-service / content-led£1,500–£3,000+Original photography or video, strategy, active community management, detailed reporting

Ad-hoc or hourly work, an audit, a single strategy session, a one-off content batch, typically runs £25 to £150 an hour depending on experience and what's involved.

What actually changes the price

Platform count matters less than most people assume. The real cost driver is who's creating the content. A package where the client supplies photos and the provider just schedules and captions them is inexpensive to deliver. A package where the provider is filming, photographing, editing and designing from nothing is considerably more labour-intensive, and the price reflects that.

Video is the single biggest factor beyond that. Producing and editing original video costs meaningfully more than static graphics, because it takes longer at every stage: filming, sound, editing, captioning, exporting for each platform's format.

Location plays a smaller role than it used to. London-based providers still tend to charge a premium, but a lot of UK social media work now happens remotely, so a business in Manchester or Cheshire isn't restricted to local pricing, and a local provider isn't automatically cheaper just for being nearby.

Outsourcing vs an in-house hire

It's worth running the comparison properly rather than assuming in-house is cheaper because there's no "agency markup." A UK social media manager's salary typically falls somewhere between roughly £28,000 and £42,000 a year, depending on experience and location. Add employer National Insurance, pension contributions, software subscriptions, a laptop and camera equipment, and management time, and the real annual cost climbs well past the headline salary.

For most small businesses, especially those needing content created rather than just scheduled, outsourcing to a freelancer or small studio works out more predictable, and often cheaper overall, than employing someone full-time to do the same job.

How to compare quotes fairly

  • Ask what's included in "content." Scheduling supplied images and producing original photography or video are very different jobs at very different prices.
  • Check posting frequency and platform count against the fee, not just the headline number.
  • Ask whether reporting is included, and how often.
  • Be cautious of unusually low quotes. If a price is well below the bands above, ask exactly what's covered before assuming it's a bargain.
  • Ask about minimum terms. Many providers require a three to six month commitment, which is worth knowing before you compare a monthly figure directly against a rolling one.

Common questions

How much does social media management cost per month in the UK?

Most small UK businesses pay somewhere between £250 and £1,500 a month, depending on how many platforms are covered, how much original content is created, and whether community management is included.

Is a freelancer or an agency cheaper for social media management?

Freelancers are usually cheaper, typically £250 to £800 a month, due to lower overheads. Boutique and regional agencies typically run £400 to £1,500 a month, usually for more capacity and a wider skill set.

Is it cheaper to hire an in-house social media manager?

Rarely, once the full cost is counted. A UK social media manager's salary typically sits between roughly £28,000 and £42,000 a year, before National Insurance, pension, software and equipment. For most small businesses, outsourcing works out more predictable and often cheaper.

What makes social media management more expensive?

Content creation, not platform count, is the biggest driver. Managing supplied content is cheap. Filming, photographing, editing and designing original content from scratch costs considerably more.
